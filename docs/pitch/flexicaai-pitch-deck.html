The problem

A clinic's day is held together by hand.

The real cost is the patient nobody called back.

Most private clinics run on four disconnected tools: a notebook for clinical notes, the receptionist's personal WhatsApp for reminders, a paper register or spreadsheet for payments, and a calculator for associate doctors' revenue shares.

Press → to reveal the punchline. The biggest loss isn't the admin time. It's the follow-up visit that never gets booked, because nobody called.

Why now
Channel

Patients already live on WhatsApp.

Capability

AI can now draft a clinical note from speech.

Buyer

The buyer is the dentist in the chair.

Three things line up. First, patients here answer WhatsApp, not email or SMS, so that is the channel we use. Second, speech-to-text and language models are now good enough to draft a structured note for a clinician to approve. Third, in this market the clinic owner is usually the practising dentist. The person who feels the paperwork is the person who signs the cheque.

Platform

Built once. Every specialty plugs in.

Scheduling, records, WhatsApp, billing and reporting are shared. Only the vocabulary, note structure, formulary and recall rules change.

70–80%

of the product is a shared core, so a new specialty is a module, not a rewrite.

This is the strategic asset. About 70–80% of the code is a specialty-neutral core. Everything specific to dentistry (the AI prompt, the drug formulary, recall rules, the tooth chart) sits in a separate module. The build enforces that boundary automatically, so the core can never come to depend on dental code.

Adding dermatology or hair transplant means writing a new module and registering it, with no changes to the core. Each new specialty is a new market on the same platform.

For a clinical product this is the question that matters. Nothing the AI writes becomes part of the record until the clinician who dictated it approves it. The AI never answers a patient's medical question.

Each clinic's data is filtered on every query, and an automated check flags any query that isn't. Access is per person and per permission. Deleted records can be restored, and every action is in the audit log. The browser never talks to the database directly.
